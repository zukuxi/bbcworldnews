Zelensky rallies EU support as Trump says Ukraine leader 'more difficult to deal with than Putin'

- Published

US President Donald Trump has renewed his sharp criticism of Volodymyr Zelensky, saying the Ukrainian leader is "more difficult to deal with than [Russian President Vladimir] Putin".

Trump, who recently made a controversial deal with Moscow to import its diesel to the American market, called Ukraine's fresh wave of strikes on Russian oil refineries a "disgrace".

Zelensky had paused the attacks for three days at Trump's request for a ceasefire on energy targets, but said on Thursday they would resume because Moscow had never stopped.

Tensions between Washington and Kyiv have escalated recently over the US-Russia diesel deal and restrictions on Ukraine's satellite communications on the battlefield.

Zelensky has not commented on Trump's latest criticism.

He arrived at an EU summit in Brussels on Friday flanked by European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and European Council President António Costa.

"Of course we supported President Trump's energy ceasefire but we've seen that Russia did not respect it, not a single minute," Von der Leyen told reporters, adding that sanctions on Russia "have to increase".

Moscow said it had never agreed to the energy ceasefire that Trump suddenly announced last week.

Costa did not mention the US, but said that "this is no time to withdraw support" to the country and "no time to relieve Russian sanctions", as the US has done in agreeing to the deal with Russia to import 300,000 tonnes of its diesel.

The summit's agenda focuses on funding for Ukraine and helping it get through another cold winter - the fifth since Russia launched its full-scale invasion in February 2022.

"Russia is trying everything to destroy the energy and water infrastructure," Von der Leyen told Zelensky before the summit began, "but we stand by your side, we support you as we did in the other winters."

Zelensky said his country wants the war to end "as quick as possible", but added that Russia is not showing signs that it wants the same thing.

"That's why we have to prepare for the winter. A winter package is not only energy, it's also defence and funding," he said.

Deadly attacks on Ukraine continued overnight, with several people killed in a Russian drone strike in the north-eastern city of Kharkiv, while some 270,000 households were without power after strikes in the western Volyn region, the local governors said.

Trump's comments about Zelensky, during an interview with US outlet Newsmax on Thursday evening, reflect the deteriorating relationship between the US and Ukraine.

The US president said he is "not a big fan of Zelensky" and that he is "not a good guy for us to deal with.

"He doesn't want to settle. He says he wants to settle, but he doesn't," Trump said.

"As you know, Putin hates Zelensky, and Zelensky hates Putin," he added. "And when you have that kind of hatred, it's very hard to get something settled."

When asked about Ukraine's renewed strikes on Russian oil refineries, Trump said it was "a disgrace".

"All he's doing is making the world oil price higher... There are a lot of targets that you can hit."

Trump is frustrated at Ukraine's continued targeting of Russian oil refineries because it affects Moscow's ability to export its diesel to America, as per the deal the US recently made with Russia, in an effort to bring down fuel prices.

Since the war with Iran started in February, US fuel and especially diesel prices have skyrocketed, souring Trump standing with the American public - something he wants to fix before next month's mid-term elections.

The diesel deal infuriated Zelensky, who called it a gift to Putin and an "investment in a war that must be ended, not prolonged."

His anger was further heightened earlier this week, when restrictions were placed on the US-owned Starlink internet network, which is critical for Kyiv's military abilities to defend itself.

On Tuesday night, when the communications network was limited, there were several deadly Russian strikes on Ukraine, with residents and the government saying there was no prior warning of the incoming attack.

The Trump administration had previously warned that it could withhold intelligence sharing - which is crucial for Ukraine's defences - if Kyiv does not stop hitting Russia's oil refineries.

Zelensky has been holding back-to-back talks with EU leaders while in Brussels. On Thursday he met Nato chief Marke Rutte, as well as the leaders of Poland, France, Denmark and Belgium.

A day earlier, he met UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham at No 10 in London.

At the summit on Friday, Zelensky planed to hold talks with the chiefs of the European Council and European Commission, and the prime ministers of Hungary, Finland, and Sweden.

Ukraine also continued its strikes against Russia overnight. The airspace above Moscow was closed for several house, causing flight delays, as 644 drones were intercepted above 26 Russian regions, the defence ministry said.

Ninety-four Ukrainian drones were shot down as they approached Moscow, the mayor said.

In the Vladimir region, east of Moscow, the governor said that Ukrainian drones were intercepted to prevent an attack on heating infrastructure in the area.

And the governor of the southern Rostov region said that Ukrainian drones hit industrial and warehouse facilities, causing a fire and injuring one person.
